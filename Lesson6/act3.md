# Assignment: 5 Real-World Examples of Central Limit Theorem (CLT)

## Definition of Central Limit Theorem (CLT)
The **Central Limit Theorem (CLT)** states that when you take sufficiently large random samples from any population—regardless of whether the population distribution is normal, skewed, or uniform—the distribution of the sample means will approximate a normal (bell-shaped) distribution.

---

### 1. Rolling a Single Die vs. Rolling Multiple Dice
* **Population:** A single fair 6-sided die has a uniform distribution (each face 1 through 6 has an equal probability of $\frac{1}{6}$).
* **CLT Application:** If a player rolls 30 dice at once, calculates their mean value, and repeats this process 1,000 times, the distribution of those sample means forms a bell-shaped curve centered around $3.5$.

### 2. Average Test Scores in a Large School System
* **Population:** Individual student scores in a state exam may be heavily skewed (e.g., clustered at very high or very low ends).
* **CLT Application:** If random samples of 50 students are selected across various schools and their average test scores are recorded, the resulting distribution of sample means will follow a normal distribution.

### 3. Call Center Handling Times
* **Population:** Call durations in customer support usually follow a highly skewed exponential distribution (most calls are short, while a few take a very long time).
* **CLT Application:** If management records the mean call duration across random batches of 40 calls per agent, the distribution of these batch averages forms a symmetrical normal curve, making performance analysis predictable.

### 4. Weight of Items in Manufacturing / Quality Control
* **Population:** Individual weights of manufactured food items (e.g., potato chip bags) vary due to equipment fluctuations.
* **CLT Application:** Quality control teams draw random samples of 35 bags from production runs and calculate their mean weight. The CLT guarantees that these sample means follow a normal distribution, allowing managers to set accurate tolerance thresholds.

### 5. Daily Step Counts on Fitness Apps
* **Population:** Daily step counts across a huge user base are non-normal and heavily right-skewed (sedentary vs. highly active users).
* **CLT Application:** If researchers take random samples of 100 users and compute each group's average daily step count, the distribution of these sample means forms a normal bell curve.
